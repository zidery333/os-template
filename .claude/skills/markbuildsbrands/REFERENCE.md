## The ideas everything hangs on

1. **Winning products are created, not found.** A product is a commodity; the
   marketing (ads + funnel) makes it win. He repeats this from his first video
   to his latest (2025-05-19 *Winning products are a lie*; 2025-11-14 *Winning
   products 2026*; 2026-07-30 *3-day case study*).
2. **Market first.** A so-so product in a hungry, hurting market beats a great
   product in a sleepy one (2025-06-03 *Print money 2025*; 2025-12-26 *Print
   money online*).
3. **You never lose money on ads, you buy data.** A failed test is a
   check-engine light: find where it broke, fix, relaunch (2025-05-19;
   2026-02-17 *Any product money printer*; 2026-06-27 *AI dropshipping 2026-06*).
4. **Creative is the targeting.** Meta's AI reads the ad itself to decide who
   sees it, so the ad does the targeting, not the settings (2025-06-25
   *Facebook ads data*; 2025-10-09 *Andromeda update*; 2026-05-11 *Meta
   algorithm*).
5. **Copy is the foundation of everything**, and copy comes from research
   (2026-01-02 *10 brutal lessons*; 2026-03-21 *8 years of marketing*).
6. **Marketing is belief change work**: move the buyer from beliefs that stop
   them to beliefs that lead to buying (2025-06-10 *AI found the product*;
   2026-05-28 *Neuroscience of buying*).
7. **The business that can spend the most to get a customer wins** (Dan
   Kennedy) — so push average order value (AOV) and lifetime value (LTV) up
   (2025-08-08 *White Monster & wifi*; 2026-03-21).
8. **Run more ads.** "You're one winning ad away" from changing your life. It
   is his slogan, merch line and sign-off (2025-07-09; 2025-11-24; 2026-01-02).
9. **AI is a multiplier, not a strategy.** It multiplies good fundamentals and
   bad ones alike (2026-04-29 *30 days of AI ads*; 2026-06-27).
10. **Money loves speed.** Launch fast, shrink feedback loops; planning is
    procrastination with a nicer name (2025-07-21 *8 laws of scale*;
    2026-03-27 *Antidote to mediocrity*).

## Markets

- **What makes a good market** (newest: 2026-04-24 *Print money with AI*;
  2026-06-11 *Claude product finder*):
  - **Urgency** — they want it fixed now. Nerve-pain device beats fishing gear.
  - **High pain**, physical or emotional. Pets: owners hate their pet's pain
    more than their own.
  - **Big total market.** "The riches are in the niches" is wrong; nine-figure
    brands sit in huge markets (weight loss, pet supplements, GLP-1 drugs).
  - A mentor's rule: sell things that help people **make more money, have more
    sex, or lose weight**.
- **His go-to list** (pick one in under 30 minutes, don't overthink): pet
  (supplements), weight loss, hair growth, anything cosmetic with a clear
  before/after, skincare, supplements, beauty, health and wellness
  (2026-04-24; 2026-06-11).
- **Sell painful problems in a passionate market** (2025-12-26). Use Maslow's
  hierarchy: the lower the need, the deeper the pain, the easier the sale;
  avoid "self-actualisation" products (2025-11-14; 2026-07-30).
- **Solve rich people's problems** if you want to get rich (2025-06-03, quoting
  Hormozi in 2025-12-26).
- **Do problem research, not product research.** Find the pain, then sell
  another product that solves the same pain; one problem fits many products
  (migraines: supplement, headband, massage gun, neck traction) (2025-06-03;
  2026-06-11).
- **Purple ocean** (not his term): blue ocean = no proven demand; red ocean =
  demand but profits competed away. Enter a proven market and carve out a
  slice you own — by demographic or by angle. Example he uses again and again:
  **Primal Queen**, beef-organ supplements aimed at women (postpartum,
  perimenopause) inside the carnivore trend (2025-07-09; 2025-11-08 *Shopify
  tier list*; 2026-03-21). Links to Peter Thiel's *Zero to One*: own 100% of a
  small market.
- **Mass desire can't be created, only channelled** (Eugene Schwartz) — don't
  try to educate a market (2025-07-21; 2026-03-21).
- **Timing test:** once everyone online calls something "the new meta", you're
  late (native statics, quiz funnels) (2026-03-21; 2025-11-08).

## Finding and "creating" winning products

### The rules a product must pass

| Rule | Newest number | Where |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Margin | Sell at **3× COGS** (cost of goods incl. shipping and fees) **and** at least **$30 gross margin** | 2026-06-11; 2026-06-27 |
| Proven | A competitor already sells it at scale: **~300k visits/month** (200–300k bare minimum), or ~$300k/month revenue | 2026-06-11; 2026-06-27 |
| Pain | Solves an immediate, deep, painful problem in a passionate market | 2026-06-27; 2026-07-30 |
| Shipping | **Fits in a shoebox**; ships anywhere for under ~$5. No fridges or furniture ("speaking from experience") | 2025-11-14; 2026-07-30 |
| Bonus | **High LTV**: consumable, repeat buy, giftable, subscription, room for upsells | 2025-11-14; 2026-07-30 |

- **Margin rule over time:** $20 minimum + 3× (2025-07-09; 2025-08-08) → $25
  (2025-11-14) → **$30, because Facebook costs keep rising** (2026-04-24;
  2026-06-11; 2026-06-27). The 2026-07-30 video says $25 "to be conservative"
  — take $30 as the current rule. A $3 product sold at $9 needs a CPA under $6:
  don't.
- **Don't add the product as an unknown.** A never-sold "unicorn" adds a
  variable; top marketers hunt proven markets (2025-07-21; 2026-03-21). "You're
  never first; you're competing for second" (2025-08-08).
- **Time box it:** product finding should take about an hour, two at most
  (2026-06-11; 2026-07-30).

### Validation (the most important step)

- **Traffic can't be faked; revenue estimators can.** He calls store-spy
  revenue tools wildly wrong, tested on his own stores (2025-11-14; 2026-06-27).
- **SimilarWeb** browser extension (about 3 free looks a day). Formula:
  **visits × conversion rate × AOV = revenue**. Use 2–3% conversion (range
  1.5–3.5%, lower for pricier things), and be conservative. Example shape:
  1.5M visits × 2% = 30,000 orders × $90 AOV ≈ $2.7M/month (2026-06-11).
- High-price exception: a $350 product can pass at 40–50k visits (2025-11-14).
- Fast growth (0 → 100k visits in a month) is a "super green flag", worth
  testing even under the bar (2026-06-27).
- Also want to see the brand **running lots of ads**, rising month over month
  — 100+ active ads, ideally 150–200+ (2025-07-09; 2026-07-14).
- **Free trick:** big news sites with native ad blocks (weather.com, Fox, CNN,
  ABC; Taboola/Outbrain "Sponsored content"). Click the ads to train the
  cookies, try incognito and VPNs; the ads lead to advertorials of brands that
  are spending. Check the advertorial domain and the store domain separately
  (2026-06-27).

### How he finds products with AI (tool order over time)

1. ChatGPT **deep research** with his product-research prompt (2025-06-10 *AI
   found the product*).
2. **GetHooked** filters → find winning ads → trace back to the brand
   (2025-07-09; 2025-08-08; 2025-11-14). Filters he uses: 4–5 star ads, still
   running, ran at least 7 days, English, brand with 100+ active ads.
3. A ChatGPT Agent validation tool, "under 30 minutes" (2025-11-14).
4. **Claude Cowork** "product finder" project: fill a brief (market, things to
   exclude, budget, skill level, quick flip vs long-term brand — he says
   long-term) (2026-04-24).
5. **Newest:** Claude + the **GetHooked MCP connector** (a plug-in that lets
   Claude search GetHooked's ad library). Step 1: a prompt lists ~30 emotional
   sub-niches; pick 5. Step 2: a prompt makes Claude scan advertisers in those
   sub-niches and report product, pain point, ad pattern, funnel and top ad.
   Then check margins, shoebox and LTV by hand (2026-06-11; 2026-07-30).

### What a failed product test usually means

- It's rarely the product. Ask *why*: ad problem or funnel problem? "This isn't
  2018 general-store dropshipping" (2026-06-11).
- **One relaunch rule:** launch, read data, fix the worst number, relaunch
  **once**; if still nowhere near break-even, cut it (2025-11-14, a "hard rule
  for most students"; ranked C, "beginners only", in 2025-11-08).
- Don't spam-test 10–100 products a day; that's 2018–2020 churn-and-burn, not
  a brand you can sell one day (2025-06-30 *Dropshipping advice tier list*).

### How much the product matters (he never settles on one number)

5% (2025-06-25) → ~50% (2025-06-30; 2025-07-09) → **last of four** after
market, ads, funnel (2025-12-26) → **~20%, with ads + funnel 80%** (2026-06-27,
newest). The "EQ sliders" picture: product, ads and funnel are sliders; a 10/10
product carries weak ads, a 5/10 product needs better ads and funnel, and
dropshipped products top out around 7/10 (2026-04-24; 2026-06-27).

## Competing: angles, perceived value, advantage

- **Ask "what am I competing on?"** before testing: product (weakest), funnel,
  ads, or angle (2025-05-19). Never copy a brand 1:1 — they have the data edge
  (pixel, account history). Find at least one advantage first; "business is
  war" (Sun Tzu) (2025-08-08).
- **Angles** = the different reasons people buy the same product (iPhone:
  tech nerds, early adopters, camera buyers). Obsess over the angle, not the
  perfect product. He has an angle-research prompt (2025-12-26; 2026-04-24).
  Worked example: senior-dog non-slip paw grips → angles: post-surgery rehab,
  special-needs dogs, new hardwood floors, **floor anxiety** (chosen).
- **POV = perception of value**: people buy perceived value, not cost. Charge
  2–3× more through branding, label, colour and above all the ads. Don't
  compete on price — race to the bottom, low-quality buyers (2025-06-30;
  2025-12-26). Don't "season the pixel" with discounts.
- **Build the ad for the top 10% of buyers** (the ones who renew and pay more);
  the creative steers who the pixel finds (2025-12-26).
- **Contrast:** study the top competitors and creators, then ask "how can I do
  the exact opposite?" When everyone zigs, zag (2026-03-21; 2026-06-27).
- **Disguise your marketing** (Gary Halbert's mail that didn't look like
  promotion) — advertorials, quizzes and native ads all descend from it
  (2026-03-21).
- **New delivery format** as product R&D: take a top supplement and change how
  it's taken (liquid collagen) (2026-04-29).

## Research: the "foundational docs"

The base of all his copy, ads, landers and quizzes. Newest description (2025-11-21 *Ugly landing
pages*; 2026-04-29; 2026-07-14):

1. **Research doc** — what customers say on forums, Reddit, YouTube and TikTok
   comments, in their own words (voice of customer), with sources; 20–60
   pages; pains and past solutions tried. Read it top to bottom.
2. **Avatar sheet** — demographics, pains, goals, emotional drivers, direct
   quotes ("gold for hooks").
3. **Offer brief** — product, awareness level, stage of sophistication, level
   of consciousness, big idea, unique mechanism (of the problem and of the
   solution), discovery story.
4. **Necessary beliefs** — 4–6 lines starting "I believe that…" the buyer must
   hold before buying.

- Made with AI deep research in about 30 minutes (ChatGPT deep research in
  2025; **Perplexity deep research "mostly" now**, 2026-07-08). Tip: have the
  AI write the deep-research prompt for you, from a competitor's sales page
  saved as a PDF (2025-07-30).
- **Pre-train a model** with the docs and reuse it: Claude Projects, ChatGPT
  custom GPTs, Gemini Gems (2026-01-16). One project per job (advertorials,
  ad scripts, image prompts) (2025-06-10).
- **Best research of all is your own customers.** His team phones customers;
  one customer's exact phrase, used word for word as a hook, became one of his
  most profitable ads (2026-03-21). Ask buyers "what almost kept you from
  buying?" (2026-01-02).
- **Never use ad comments** for research or FAQs — "the lowest of the low hang
  out there"; it's survivorship bias (2026-01-02; 2026-07-08).
- **AI customer avatar:** load the docs, tell the AI to be the customer, then
  talk to it by voice on a walk; make one per angle (2026-03-21).

## Copywriting

- **Copy is the number-one skill in e-commerce**; copywriters who become brand
  owners "destroy everybody" (2026-01-02). "Salesmanship in print" (Halbert,
  2025-08-04).
- **Time split** from top copywriters: **80% research, 5% writing, 15%
  "chiefing"** (his word for editing: reread through the customer's eyes and a
  copywriter's) (2026-03-21). Read it aloud 5–7 times until it flows like a
  greased slide (2026-04-17).
- **Clarity over cleverness.** "A confused mind doesn't buy." Aim for a
  5th-grade reading level, never above 8th; check with hemingwayapp.com
  (2026-03-21; 2026-04-17; 2026-07-08).
- **Sell the outcome**, not features or even benefits: features → benefits →
  the emotional state after (2025-08-22; 2026-07-08).
- **People buy on emotion and justify with logic** (Zig Ziglar). Ad shape:
  emotional hook → logical proof → call to action. "Hook them on the feeling,
  close them with proof" (2025-12-26; 2026-05-28).
- **Stages of awareness** (unaware → problem → solution → product → most
  aware): write to one stage and one person (2026-03-21).
- **Stages of sophistication** (Schwartz): most markets are at stage 4+, where
  you compete on the mechanism itself; skincare is near 5 (2026-03-21;
  2026-07-08).
- **Unique mechanism**: of the problem (why everything failed) and of the
  solution (why this works). Example shape: low iron in women = problem;
  absorbable animal iron = solution (2025-08-22; 2025-11-21).
- **Strong words:** "without" ("get X without Botox, clinics, needles", listed
  by how common they are) and "you" (second only to the person's name)
  (2026-04-29; 2026-07-08; 2026-05-28).
- **Depth beats everything** — the "iceberg" of five layers (2026-05-28
  *Neuroscience of buying*):
  1. Conscious thoughts — "enter the conversation already in their mind"
     (Robert Collier). Only ~5% of decisions, he says.
  2. Emotions — Damasio's patient who couldn't decide without emotion.
  3. Beliefs — the brain updates only on surprise; replace each limiting
     belief ("I've tried everything", "it's genetics").
  4. Identity — **identity labelling**: keep telling the reader who they are
     ("you're the kind of owner who…"), from hypnotic-copy training.
  5. Level of consciousness — David Hawkins' map. **Bell-curve method**: meet
     them at their low level (guilt, fear, grief), lift them to hope and
     courage, then bring them back down at the call to action so the
     "emotional delta" (the felt swing) is biggest. He admits this layer is
     not neuroscience.
- **Swipe the philosophy, not the copy.** Pull out *why* an ad worked and feed
  that to AI. Blind swiping "works until it doesn't" (2026-03-21). See "Where
  he changed his mind" on swiping.
- **AI and copy:** Claude is his copy tool ("Claude is king", 2025-06-10);
  write in steps (load research → analyse the swipe → rewrite), not one giant
  prompt (2026-07-14). Then always chief the output; never run raw AI copy.
  He no longer really employs copywriters (2026-03-21). "Copywriting alone" is
  now a D-tier way to make money; with AI it's B (2025-08-04).

## Hooks

- **The hook is the first 3–5 seconds** (or the first line, subject line,
  headline). Spend most of your time there; he says 90% of a video ad's success
  is the hook (2025-07-21; 2026-03-21; 2026-04-11).
- **A video hook has three parts:** copy **~80%** (70–80% in 2026-04-11),
  scroll-stopper visual ~15%, audio ~5% (2025-08-22; 2026-03-21). Scroll-stopper
  test: would your mom say "what did I just watch?"
- **Seven-point checklist** (more ticks = better; aim for at least 3–4, don't
  cram all 7): curiosity (most important), call out the pain, promise a
  solution, specificity, credibility, time frame, simplicity (2025-08-22;
  2025-11-21; 2026-07-08).
- **PIG = "Punch In the Gut"** (Chris Haddad) for leads; his standard note to
  writers is "make it punch harder". Works for emotional markets only
  (2025-08-08; 2026-03-21).
- **"I was just like you, but worse"** — first-person story hook; the viewer
  thinks "if she fixed a worse case, I can" (2026-04-29).
- **Static images:** the image itself is the hook. Two kinds: transformation
  (nightmare → dream) or nightmare only (2025-07-30).
- **Hook rate** = % still watching after 3 seconds. KPI **50%+**, non-negotiable
  (2025-06-25; 2026-02-17; 2026-11-02).
- **AI hook pipeline** (2025-12-10 *Unlimited hooks*): ChatGPT agent mode pulls
  20 customer quotes → Claude (with the research docs) turns them into 20
  hooks/headlines. Earlier: a Claude project loaded with the docs, then "write
  me 10 hooks" and push back ("too long") (2025-08-22).
- **Comedy openers rarely scale** on cold traffic (exceptions: True Classic,
  Purple) (2026-05-28).

## Ad creatives

### Video ad structure (2025-08-22 *Video ads 2025*; 2026-04-11)

**Hook → body (mechanism + how it works + future pacing) → CTA 1 → social proof
→ CTA 2.** 1–2 minutes.

- CTA 1: simple instruction + outcome; catches people already sold.
- Social proof: every testimonial must pre-handle an objection or plant a
  belief; have customers film specific ones.
- CTA 2: for sceptics — guarantee, real scarcity, offer (free gifts). These
  three are ~90% of any sales close.

### Direct vs indirect ads

- **Direct** = "buy my stuff": offer-heavy statics, UGC; best for warm and
  retargeting traffic, profitable at lower scale (2026-01-16; 2026-07-08).
- **Indirect** = the viewer doesn't feel sold: **native statics** (a picture
  that looks like a normal post + long story-style primary text that does the
  selling), news-headline images, "read the article". Cheaper clicks, higher
  CTR, lower conversion; they scale most on cold traffic but need a pre-sell
  page (advertorial, listicle, quiz) before the sales page (2026-01-16;
  2026-04-24; 2026-07-08).
- **Long-form direct-response statics**: one image + 1,000+ words of story
  copy (2025-09-26 *37 secrets*).
- **Formats he names as working:** founder ads, interview ads, podcast clips
  (now tired for younger viewers), AI UGC, AI animation, native statics, mini
  VSLs, long VSLs (8–12 minutes were big in 2024–25) (2025-09-26; 2026-07-08;
  2026-07-30).

### Rules for creatives

- **Ugly ads = pretty profits.** Clear beats beautiful; many of his best ads
  "look like garbage". He even lowers video resolution so ads look native
  (2026-03-21; 2026-11-02).
- **Aim for a winning message, not a winning ad.** Find the message (often the
  top angle) fast with cheap image ads, then turn it into every format
  (2026-04-11; 2026-04-29).
- **The new moat is the thinking behind each ad**, and turning that thinking
  into a system — not tools or volume, which anyone has (2026-04-29).
- **Volume:** one brand tests 20–30 creatives a day (2025-06-30); his minimum
  is **25 net-new concepts a week** (2026-01-02).
- **Before/after images** risk rejection and bans; illustrated/animated
  before/afters get approved more often (2025-06-10; 2025-07-30). Treat this as
  ad-policy risk, not a trick.
- **Look backwards for formats** — decades of TV commercials, not Twitter,
  which is 6 months behind (2026-04-29).

### After you find a winner (newest: 2026-11-02 *Creative rat race*)

- **The "creative rat race":** people pile micro-variations (new hooks, new
  scroll-stoppers) on a winner, none beat it, the profit burns, the winner
  dies, back to zero. Worse, they then blame the product or lander.
- **Creative tier list F → S:** a creative's tier is how much it can spend
  profitably (F = unprofitable; capped at $5k, $10k, $1k/day…; S = seven
  figures). Don't force a $5k-cap ad to $20k; hunt higher-tier ads.
- **Winning variable extraction:** break winners into variables — awareness
  level, sophistication stage, mechanism, authority figure, format, style,
  shock level, camera angle, hook wording, the presenter's age, gender and
  ethnicity, background. Keep a library and stack winning variables into new
  concepts. S-tier ads are created, not found.
- **Test like a scientist:** look at data → guess why → change one variable →
  test → repeat. Example: high hook rate but poor sales → keep the hook, test a
  new body.
- His big winners are usually not swipes; his swipes rarely beat his controls.

## AI image ads

Why images: fast, cheap, lots of tests, no editors; some of his best-ever ads
were images; they tire faster than video but not before $10–50k spend on one
(2025-07-30; 2026-01-16; 2026-04-11). Video is more scalable and usually
converts better ("attention in isolation") (2026-04-11). *Was:* "images are
cheaper than video" — in 2026-01-02 he says some of his images cost more than
his best videos; test it.

**Workflow (newest: 2026-04-11 *Higgsfield ads*; 2026-01-16 *AI image ads*):**

1. Foundational docs first.
2. **Swipe or new concept.** Swipe: GetHooked → 4–5 star image ads, still
   active, 7+ days, English → download. New concept: an "ideation" prompt.
   Both equally strong; most people only swipe.
3. Upload the ad to a **Claude Project** (or a Gemini Gem) that holds the docs
   and his instructions, with no extra words. It explains why the ad worked and
   writes a text-to-image prompt for your brand.
4. **Higgsfield → Nano Banana Pro**, 9:16 (or 1:1), 4 variations, and always
   upload a **PNG of your product on white**.

   editable (2026-04-29).

## Meta ads

### How the algorithm works (2025-10-09 *Andromeda update*; 2026-05-11 *Meta algorithm*)

- Each ad slot is decided in about **200 milliseconds**, from tens of millions
  of ads. Four steps: **retrieval** (is this ad relevant to this person? —
  decided mostly from the creative) → **light ranking** → **heavy ranking**
  (the couple of hundred you really compete with) → **auction**.
- **Total value = bid × estimated action rate + user value/ad quality.**
  Estimated action rate = estimated CTR × estimated click-to-conversion rate.
  So soft metrics (CTR above all) are half the formula; Meta rewards high CTR
  with cheaper reach. The user-value part is Meta guarding itself from scammy
  advertisers.
- **Andromeda** = Meta's AI retrieval system (article December 2024, fully live
  around July 2025). Built because AI ads flooded the platform. It groups
  similar-looking ads together, so near-identical variations aren't tested
  separately. Hence: creative diversity, creative = targeting.
- "Facebook is a data platform, not an ad platform." It's a public company that
  favours big advertisers; **ban waves follow its earnings reports**;
  identical ads get approved for one person and rejected for another.
  **Don't take Meta's suggestions** ("lower your cost per result by 11%") —
  they're there to take your money (2026-05-11).

### Testing structure (newest: 2026-05-11; 2026-07-30; built on 2025-11-26 *Testing after Andromeda*)

- **One CBO** (budget set at campaign level) for new ads. **Each ad set = one
  net-new concept or format** (e.g. AI animation, native static, founder ad,
  mini podcast VSL). **3–5 ads per ad set** = big variations of that concept
  (not just a new hook — small variations let one ad hog the spend).
  "Shotgun, not sniper."
- **Budget:** at least **$10/day per ad** in the CBO (9 ads → $90/day); never a
  CBO under **$50/day** — "if you can't afford $50/day you shouldn't run paid
  ads" (2025-11-26).
- Images and videos in separate ad sets or campaigns (videos eat images'
  spend); simplest is one or the other. He usually tests with images.
- **Settings:** objective Sales; optimise for **Purchase** only (any other
  objective fills the pixel with junk data, 2025-06-30); 7-day click / 1-day
  view; start the next day 6:00 a.m. Eastern; US; ages 18–65+, both genders
  unless the product is for one; Advantage+ placements, but **exclude Audience
  Network** at account level; **completely broad**, at most one exclusion of
  past buyers. At ad level turn **off** every creative enhancement, "optimize
  website destination", promo codes, site links, image generation; the link
  must match the copy exactly. CTA "Learn more" (a slight lift over "Shop
  now"). Multi-advertiser ads may stay on (2025-11-26; 2026-07-30).
- **Leave the first page alone:** never split-test the page your ads land on
  first (Meta crawls it; you wreck the learning). Send new post IDs to a new
  URL with the same creatives instead (2026-07-08). Split test later pages
  50/50.
- **Kill fast early, be lenient after proof** (a creative profitable over
  ~$500 spend, a lander over ~$1,000). He cut a losing lander after $300–400
  (2026-07-30).
- Fresh start for each test brand: new pixel, ad account, Shopify account
  (2026-07-30).

**Concepts vs variations — how his view moved:**

- 2017–18 and up to mid-2025: one new concept + 3–5 variations; **80%
  variations / 20% new concepts** (2025-06-30; 2025-07-30).
- After Andromeda: **flipped to 80% new concepts / 20% variations**, up to 100%
  new on a new launch; stop "one body, five hooks" (2025-09-26; 2025-10-09;
  2025-11-26).
- **Newest:** variations are **not** dead — validate a broad concept first,
  then variations inside it give more "at-bats"; one variation was a top
  spender in his account (2026-01-02; 2026-05-11). After a winner, extract
  variables rather than pile on micro-variations (2026-11-02).

### Media buying

- His buying is mostly "boring": broad, Advantage+ audiences, CBOs, Advantage+
  Shopping campaigns, "let it rip"; maybe 10–20% "ninja stuff" (cost caps,
  bid caps, budget surfing) (2025-10-09).
- **Media buying is maybe 10–20% of success**, creative ~90% on Facebook
  (2025-10-09; 2025-11-08). *But* "media buying is not nothing" — learn
  structure, testing, naming, tracking, when to cut or scale; people saying it's
  dead "were never media buyers" (2026-01-02). Think like an entrepreneur, not
  a media buyer: you can't media-buy your way from $1k to $10k a day
  (2026-05-11).
- **Bidding:** most run lowest cost; he's seen six figures a day profitable on
  lowest cost. Cost caps (max per customer) and bid caps are tools for a
  specific problem. Cost caps worked well for creative testing (2025-09-26;
  2026-05-11).
- **Tactics from 2025-09-26 *37 secrets*:** "surf scaling" (raise budgets
  through a good day, reset at 5 p.m. or midnight); CBO with bid caps;
  automatic rules to kill ads on soft metrics (e.g. CPC 3× account average);
  test only top placements and one country first; test pages (brand,
  personality, interest pages; VSLs often do better from interest pages);
  whitelisted influencer pages; fire an extra pixel event after an upsell so
  Meta looks for bigger spenders; omnichannel retargeting (Google, YouTube,
  Bing, TikTok, Snapchat) for up to ~20% more.
- **Don't touch what's working** — the golden rule; double down beside it, not
  on it (2025-11-08).
- **Learning phase:** "your ads are always learning"; results swing like a
  sine wave from $50 to $1k/day and above. **When in doubt, zoom out**: judge
  on 7+ days. Want consistency? Advertise on Google (2026-05-11).
- **Scaling too fast** breaks payment processors (PayPal, Stripe, Shopify
  Payments bans), ad accounts and suppliers; he prefers steady growth
  (2025-06-30).
- **"Quantum leaps":** brands stall around $2–3k/day, $15–20k/day, $100k/day.
  Small tests won't break those ceilings; take a big swing — new funnel
  structure, new account structure, new format (2025-11-08).

### Tracking and infrastructure

- After **iOS 14** (2020–21), Meta's numbers are off by 5–50%. He doesn't trust
  Meta's CPA at all; uses third-party attribution: **Triple Whale**, **Hyros**,
  Wicked Reports, WeTrack, or a vibe-coded one (2026-03-21; 2026-05-11).
- Send server-side data (**Conversions API**). EMQ (event match quality) is
  "overrated" — he had 9.8 with no clear effect (2026-05-11).
- **"Bulletproof" setup:** several business managers, ad accounts, pages and
  pixels, so you're back up within a day after a ban; backup and agency
  accounts (2025-09-26; 2025-11-08; 2026-07-30). He was banned on day 2 of a
  test despite a warmed account and zero rejected ads.

## Reading the data (KPIs)

Every number is a **check-engine light**. Ask "**Where is the breakdown
occurring?**" — he drills it into every hire. **Hose picture** (from Hormozi):
find the kink. You don't need every number perfect; averaging ~75% across the
sliders is profitable (2025-06-25; 2026-02-17 *Any product money printer*,
the fullest list).

**Soft metrics (ad side):**

| Metric | Newest KPI (US) | Notes / older numbers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Link CPC (cost per *link* click, not "CPC all") | **~$1.50**; $2–4 = something's wrong; worldwide under $1 | Check this first. *Was* under $1 (2025-05-19) |
| Link CTR | **3% minimum, ~4% ideal**; under 3% = serious creative problem | *Was* 2% (2025-05-19). CTR(all) is usually 2–3× link CTR; a big gap = engaged but not buying |
| CPM | No hard rule; **over ~$80 is high**; $40–50 normal | *Was* under $20 (2025-05-19), $30–50 (2025-06-25). $100–150 steady = Meta dislikes the ads or the account |
| Hook rate (3-second views ÷ impressions) | **50%+** | Video only |
| Hold rate (he uses 50% video views) | **25%+** for short ads | Not for long VSLs |
| Frequency (cold traffic) | **under 1.5** | *Was* "around 2 means something's broken" (2025-06-25) |

**Hard metrics (funnel side):**

| Metric | Newest KPI | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Conversion rate | **3–5%**: $20–30 product 5%, mid 4%, $90–100+ 3% | Under 3% = not good (complex funnels can go lower) |
| Add-to-cart rate (of first-page visitors) | **10%** (8–9% OK for pricier products) | Low = weak page or "curiosity clickers" from the ad |
| Add-to-cart → purchase | **30%** (3 of 10) | Low = checkout problem: buy from your own store first, check payment processor approval rates, simplify |
| AOV | **≥ 2× front-end price** | Plain Shopify: aim 20–50% above price (2026-06-11). 1:1 is "2018 e-com" |
| LTV : AOV | **3 : 1** | LTV = AOV means nobody comes back |
| CAC (cost to get a customer) | **under 50% of AOV** (≈ 2× ROAS) | "Small CAC is king" |
| LTV : CAC | **3 : 1 minimum**; 7–10 : 1 for high-ticket | 2026-03-21 |
| Quiz completion | **~30%** start → sales page (25% at scale) | 2025-08-15 |
| Dry-test opt-in rate | **~10%** (8–9% for $100+ products) | 2025-09-16 |

- **Common combinations** (2026-02-17): high CPM + high CPC + low CTR = crowded
  market and bad creatives. Good CTR (6%) + low add-to-cart (3%) = curiosity
  clickers or a terrible funnel ("content creators' main problem in e-com").
  Everything good but AOV terrible = bad unit economics.
- **90% of problems are ad problems** (2026-06-11). Rarely only the funnel;
  good ads survive a weak funnel (2026-02-17).
- **Don't chase vanity CTR** with curiosity native statics that don't sell —
  "you're not a content creator" (2026-06-11).
- **Judge page tests on earnings per view**, not conversion rate, because AOV
  moves too (2026-07-08).
- **Know your numbers before launch:** break-even CAC (price − cost) and
  break-even ROAS; he aims for 20%+ net margin (2025-07-21; 2025-11-14).
  High break-even ROAS (1.7–2.0) is a trap: holding 3× ROAS at high spend on
  Facebook "doesn't happen" (2026-07-30).
- **"Intuition isn't real" in marketing** — it's pattern recognition from
  data; at ~20,000 hours he's still wrong all the time (2026-03-21).

## Funnels

### Basic vs "Chad" funnel

- **"Basic" funnel:** ad → Shopify product page (PDP) → checkout → thank-you.
  Can work with great ads and a great PDP, and for subscriptions, but the ad
  must do all the educating and Shopify upsells are weak (2025-11-14;
  2026-06-27). Going straight to the PDP is like asking someone home in your
  first sentence on a first date — his favourite analogy (2025-11-21;
  2025-12-26; 2026-04-24).
- **"Chad" funnel** (his name): indirect ad (long-copy image or mini VSL) →
  **pre-sell page** (advertorial, listicle or quiz) → **sales page** (sells the
  outcome) → **order page** (bumps, quantity breaks) → payment → **OTO 1**
  (one-time offer after paying) → OTO 2 or a downsell (2025-11-14; 2025-11-21;
  2026-06-27). The ad only has to "sell the click".
- He runs the Chad funnel **off Shopify** — on **Funnelish** or **Checkout
  Champ** (or custom) — because Shopify's post-purchase upsells are "cooked"
  (2025-08-08; 2026-06-27). But in his newest case study he ran **100% Shopify,
  ad → PDP, free Debut theme**, to prove theme obsession is noise
  (2026-07-30). Both work; the Chad funnel wins on economics.
- **Match traffic temperature to the page:** retargeting ads don't need a
  four-page funnel; native/indirect ads do ("law of consumption": the more
  someone has consumed, the closer to the offer you can send them)
  (2026-07-08).
- **Funnel diversity matters more than creative diversity** — his "unpopular
  opinion". A better funnel raises the floor and ceiling of every creative,
  like a rising tide (2026-04-29). (Compare 2025-11-08: creative is 90% of
  Facebook success. Both stand: creative wins the auction, funnel decides how
  far it scales.)
- **Funnel fashions pass:** advertorials, then VSLs, then quizzes (2025-08-15).
- **For beginners:** get a store to ~$1k/day first, then build funnels
  (2025-07-09).

### Pre-sell pages

- **Advertorial** = ad + editorial; looks like an article, sells
  subconsciously. Claude workflow: docs + a competitor's running advertorial →
  rewrite → chief → images; ~5 minutes; host on Shopify, ClickFunnels,
  Funnelish or your own domain via Cloudflare (2026-04-24).
- **Listicle** ("5 reasons why…") built with Claude (2026-04-17).
- He remains "bullish on advertorials" (2025-08-08).

### Quiz funnels (2025-08-15 *Quiz funnels*)

- They work by putting buyers in an emotionally primed state, not by
  "personalisation" or magic AOV.
- **Every question must do at least one of three things:** micro-commitment
  (each tap is a small yes), **seeding** (plant a belief, often with info cards
  between questions), **pre-handling an objection** ("how fast do you expect
  results?"). Else cut it.
- First question = gentle, never awkward (don't open by asking older women
  their age); go from gentle to personal ("gradualisation", Schwartz). No more
  than 2–3 of the same question type in a row; avoid typing questions; make
  taps satisfying.
- **8–12 questions** is the sweet spot (3–20 possible). Watch drop-off per
  question: 1–2% is normal; fix or remove a spike.
- End with name + email "for your results", then a 5–7 email sequence, ideally
  split by answers. Ad CTA can be "take the quiz".
- Build: competitor quiz screenshots + docs + the three rules → AI writes it.
- **Newest view: C tier** — still runs them and they "still print", but the hype
  has worn them down and they won't save a bad offer (2025-11-08). *Was* the
  hot funnel of the moment.

### Sales pages / landing pages

- **Only two numbers decide a page: conversion rate and AOV.** Ugly is fine
  ("ugly landers = pretty profits") (2025-11-21; 2026-04-17).
- A page = **copy + media + design**; copy matters most, design only if it adds
  friction. Clarity first. Conversion is a game of **subtraction** (2025-11-21;
  2026-07-08).
- **Prefer a custom sales page over a template PDP** (built with Claude, Replo
  or GemPages) — you control how the product is framed before the price
  (2026-07-08).
- **Work on mobile** (95% of traffic) and spend ~90% of the effort **above the
  fold**, whose only job is to make them scroll (2026-07-08).
- **Sales page checklist** (2025-11-21): benefit headline (most leave if it
  fails) → urgency → scarcity (ethical wording) → guarantee/risk reversal (be
  creative; don't just add days) → mechanism → social proof (also scattered
  through the page) → FAQs → credibility (not always a doctor; in health the
  medical system is often "the enemy" to the buyer).
- **Headline formula:** outcome + time frame + mechanism; sub-headline with
  "without" + the alternatives (2026-07-08).
- **Image carousel order** (not a hard rule, 2026-07-08): hero product shot →
  before/after ("nothing sells like a before and after") → top objection
  handler + benefit bullets naming every specific problem → (comparison,
  research, risk reversal in any order) → testimonial collage.
- **Every image must install a belief or remove an objection**, and match its
  copy section (2026-04-17; 2026-04-24). Ad images and page images differ.
- **Testimonials** must match the buyer's age, gender and ethnicity, and each
  must do a job (2026-07-08).
- **Education section:** authority, ingredient research (show real studies),
  how it works (GIFs), who it's for, comparison; put social proof where people
  drop (2026-07-08).
- **His current experiment:** remove every CTA except the bottom one, to force
  people to read first. Unproven; most disagree (2026-07-08).
- **Urgency/scarcity: ethically.** Best urgency is real — holiday sales
  (2025-11-21; 2026-07-08). Note he also shows pages with "most likely fake"
  timers as examples; fake urgency can break consumer and ad rules.
- **Lander workflow with Claude** (2026-04-17): swipe page as PDF → copy
  (docs → analyse swipe → write) → images (Claude writes prompts → Higgsfield)
  → design ("base it on the swipe"; fix structure before details) → brand kit
  (**Firecrawl** "branding" scrape of a URL → JSON → Claude) → chief → deploy.
  He claims this page went from 0 to $5k/day in about 5 days *(his claim)*.

### Order page and offer

- Biggest drop-off is always the order/checkout page — keep it dead simple and
  clear (2025-11-21).
- **Order bumps** (e.g. $10–20 at ~50% take), expedited shipping, shipping
  insurance, digital products (2025-11-21).
- **Offer stack:** quantity breaks only if multiples make sense (else bundles);
  discount at each level; mark one "most popular"; add a decoy priced close to
  the top tier; every free gift must remove an objection or speed the outcome
  (2026-07-08).
- Front-end offers: BOGO, buy 2 get 2, free gift (2025-09-26).

### OTOs — "the most powerful step in the funnel"

- Right after paying, buyers are in the most emotional state they'll ever be in
  (post-purchase anxiety) (2025-08-08; 2025-11-21; 2026-06-27).
- OTO 1 can be **10× the front-end price** ($50 → $500). Measure AOV
  contribution, not take rate: $500 at 10% take = +$50 AOV (2025-11-21). Newest:
  upsell $80–300, not $20 (2026-06-27).
- Format: a near-empty page with a video ("Wait, your order isn't complete…")
  or a short text sales letter.
- **What to sell:** more of the same (extra months' supply) or, his favourite,
  **"solve one problem, create another"** (lost the weight → now keep it off)
  (2025-11-21).
- In many of his funnels most of the AOV and LTV comes after purchase
  (2025-11-08).

### Dry testing (2025-09-16 *Dry testing*)

- Test demand **before** building a store, finding a supplier or holding stock:
  docs → AI copy (~30 minutes) → a "vibe-coded lander" (his term) on
  **builder.io**, published on a subdomain → ads → the buy button becomes an
  **email opt-in**. ~10% opt-in = build it; under that, drop it. Example shape:
  three products in a day at 5%, 4%, 12% → build the 12%.
- **Risk he doesn't discuss:** you collect buy-intent sign-ups for a product
  that doesn't exist yet. Tell people the truth, and check ad rules.

- **Shopify is great for conversion rate and weak for funnels and post-purchase**

- **Back end:** email and SMS flows add 20–30% revenue with no extra ad spend

- **Geo funnels:** translate a working US funnel (language, currency, culture)
  into a less crowded country (2025-09-26).
- **Reddit and comment sections:** seed real customer posts and reviews,
  reply to every ad comment to answer objections (2025-09-26).

## Money maths: AOV, LTV, CAC

- **Dan Kennedy:** whoever can spend the most to acquire a customer wins. Ad
  platforms are auctions; even 10% more lets you take customers (2025-07-21;
  2025-08-08; 2026-03-21; 2026-07-30).
- **Three economies** (2026-03-21): ad economics (cheapest traffic *that
  buys* — not $0.50 CPMs where nobody buys), funnel economics (conversion ×
  AOV), back-end economics (LTV: rebills every 30/60/90 days, or ascension
  from a $5k to a $10k programme). "LTV is where the real money is."
- **AOV : CAC at least 2 : 1** at launch (2025-07-21). AOV aim ≥ 2× the price,
  over $100 for scaling (2025-06-03; 2026-02-17). Example shape: same
  conversion rate, competitor AOV $50 vs yours $100 → they break even at $30
  CPA, you at $80 (2025-08-08).
- **Front-end vs back-end:** he competes on front-end (very high AOV); big
  brands often lose money on the first order to win subscribers, which needs
  capital (2025-06-30; 2025-11-08). Subscriptions: watch churn (2025-08-04).
- "It's 2026 now" — upsells used to be optional (2–5× ROAS on one product);
  now they're required (2026-02-17).
- **Margins are one of the few things fully in your control** (2026-06-27).

## Branded dropshipping, start to finish

His path, put together from his newest videos (2026-04-24 *Print money with
AI*; 2026-06-11; 2026-06-27; 2026-07-30; 2026-11-02). His claims of speed: idea
to running ads in under 24 hours with AI; 0 → $1k/day in 3 days in one case
study *(his claim)*.

1. **Pick a market** from the list (under 30 minutes).
2. **Pick 5 painful sub-niches** (Claude prompt).
3. **Find a proven product** (Claude + GetHooked MCP, or ad libraries, or
   native ads on news sites). Max ~1–2 hours.
4. **Validate:** SimilarWeb traffic ≥ 200–300k/month, rising ads; margin 3×
   and $30+; shoebox; LTV potential.

## Habits, focus and health

**Focus system** (2026-02-04 *Focus*; 2026-04-21 *Staring at a wall*):

1. **Pre-load:** sit facing a wall with no phone, music or food — 10 minutes

   work (2026-04-21, newest). The brain compares; if you scrolled first, work
   feels dull. Most people have "a stimulation problem, not a discipline
   problem." It is not a "dopamine detox" — he says that idea isn't real.
2. **Activate** with a unique trigger used only for that thing: beta-alanine
   before the gym (now he tingles walking there); bone broth only before deep
   work (2026-02-04). Earlier: coffee + specific music in a blacked-out room
   (2025-08-25; 2025-11-06).

   deadlines (Parkinson's law).
4. **Exit:** breaks of 50 on / 10 off, with nothing stimulating — "fake breaks"
