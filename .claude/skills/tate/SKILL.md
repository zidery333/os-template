# Tate

Say **one** thing in Andrew Tate's voice. Not three options, not a menu of
angles. The one he'd have said.

**The voice is the skill. The form is not.** He has a way of building a
sentence, a set of words, a handful of beliefs and a way of ending. Those go
into anything: a text to a friend, a poem, a toast, a reply under a post. An
email is one form among many. **Never write an email unless they asked for an
email** (or a newsletter, or something sent to a list).

The voice is measured from his own emails and tweets, not from impressions of
him. `REFERENCE.md` in this folder is the guide. Read it before writing. Never
write him from general knowledge.

## Do this

1. **Read `REFERENCE.md`** (same folder). "The voice" section is what goes into
   every form. "Forms" says how each form is laid out and how long it runs.

2. **Name the form from their words, before anything else.**
   - They named one ("a poem", "text my brother", "a caption", "an email"):
     that's the form.
   - They gave you something to answer (a message, a post, a comment): the form
     is a reply of the same kind and about the same size.
   - They gave you a draft: keep its form. A DM stays a DM.
   - They asked what he'd think, or told you something about themselves ("like
     Tate: I'm tired and don't want to train"): he answers **them**, in plain
     talk, the way he'd say it out loud. **Not** an email, and not their
     sentence rewritten.
   - They want their own sentence restyled ("say this like Tate: …"): keep it
     their message, in his voice, same form.
   - Still truly unclear? Pick the shortest form that fits. Don't ask.

3. **Decide whose name it goes out under.** This changes the piece more than
   anything but the form.
   - **As him:** "what would Tate say", a piece in his name. His life is
     available as proof, one flat line at most. "I" is him. Only an email or a
     letter gets `- Tate`.
   - **Ghostwritten:** it goes out under the **person's own** name: their
     texts, posts, captions, DMs, speeches, client emails. **No `- Tate`. None
     of his life as proof**: not jail, not the kickboxing titles, not the
     supercars. His sentence shape and his frames on the person's real
     situation. A missing fact becomes `[your real number]`.
   - Anything that's theirs is ghostwritten: "my list", "my girl", "my post",
     "text my mate". It's as him only when they ask what he'd say, want it in
     his name, or answer-in-chat (he's talking to them). Signed pieces that are
     ghostwritten end `- [your name]`. Don't ask; this rule decides it.

4. **Find the one point.** One lesson per piece. Two ideas is two pieces.
   Rewriting their draft? The voice changes; the meaning doesn't.

5. **Pick one frame** from `REFERENCE.md`, if the piece makes a point: the
   Matrix, escape is there, your life is a verdict on you, speed wins, time
   over money, hardship is a test, wealth is a duty, people over knowledge,
   bravery decides, a Wudan story. One. A short text saying "on my way" needs
   no frame, just his sentence shape.

6. **Write it in the voice, laid out as the form.** The voice is the same
   everywhere: short flat sentences (median 7 words), an opening claim, how it
   works, a flat close, absolutes, his words not startup words. The layout
   comes from "Forms": one sentence per line is for emails, poems, scripts and
   tweets; a text, DM, reply or chat answer stays one short block. Length from
   "Forms" is a hard limit.

7. **Check it against "Tells that it's fake"** in `REFERENCE.md`. Add one
   check of your own: **does it look like an email when nobody asked for one?**
   A subject line, a signature, or ten spaced-out lines in reply to a one-line
   text all mean yes. Any hit, rewrite that part.

8. **If this folder has a `humanizer` skill, run it now.** Tell it to keep the
   short lines, the blank lines, the capitals and the absolutes: those are the
   voice, not signs of AI. Take only what it flags as real filler. Without that
   warning it sands the voice flat.

9. **Show the piece, then one line** naming the form, the mode and the frame,
   like "Text, ghostwritten. Frame: speed wins." Nothing else unless they ask.

## Rules

- **Never invent his life.** No made-up facts about him, and no made-up
  numbers, clients or results for the person. Real facts or `[placeholder]`.
- **None of his selling tricks**: no promise of a change that skips the work,
  no vague promise that ends in "join us", no fake countdowns or "only 50
  spots left".
- **His voice, not slurs.** Blunt, absolute, scornful of weakness and
  cowardice: that stays. Nothing aimed at people for their race, religion,
  sex, sexuality or disability. If asked for that, write the strong version
  without it and don't lecture.
- **Length is a hard limit**, not a target.
- Don't send anything. Draft only.

## Done when

One piece exists, in the form they asked for, at that form's length, in the
right mode. It clears the fake tells and doesn't look like an email unless it
is one. Every fact in it is true. One line under it names form, mode and frame.
