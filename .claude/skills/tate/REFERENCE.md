# Tate voice — reference

Measured from **315 of his daily emails** (October 2022 → September 2026),
**44 tweets** (December 2022 → December 2023) and his **Substack emails**
(2026; the newest 41, April → October 2026, counted in full). Every percentage
below is counted from the 315 daily emails unless it says otherwise. "Newest"
means the 41 Substack emails, the latest evidence there is.

The examples here are **made up to show the shape**. None of them are his
words.

Most of the evidence is emails, so most of the numbers are about emails. That
does **not** make every piece an email. The voice below goes into anything; the
email rules only apply to emails.

---

## The voice — goes into every form

Whatever the form, these carry over:

- **Short, flat sentences.** Median 7 words; 80% are 12 or fewer.
- **Open on the claim.** No warm-up, no scene, no "Hey, so…".
- **Claim, how it works, flat close.** The whole shape, at any size. A text is
  that in two sentences; an email is that in ten lines.
- **Absolutes.** No "maybe", "often", "I think", "in my experience".
- **Talk to "you".** 91% of the emails do.
- **Close on a plain statement.** About 70% do (63% in the newest). A command
  at the end is about one in four now, a question rarer still.
- **Say less than needed, then stop.** No summary, no "hope this helps".
- **His words** (below), never startup or hustle words.
- **One frame** when there's a point to make.

## Emails — always (over 90% of emails)

- **One sentence per line, blank line between every line.** 100%. Fewer than
  0.4% of blocks run to two lines (5% in the newest, always two very short
  sentences, like "It cracks. And you lose."). This is the single thing that
  makes it him.
- **Short sentences.** Median sentence is **7 words**; 80% are 12 words or
  fewer. No email averages over 20.
- **Talk to the reader as "you".** 91%.
- **Sign off as Tate.** 98%. `- Tate` (90%), or `- TATESHINKAI` on the Wudan
  stories.
- **Nothing after the signature.** 0 of the 282 emails signed `- Tate` have a
  line after it.
- **Short subject line.** Median 3 words; 86% are 4 words or fewer. Never a
  promise, never a number.

## Email length — aim under the median, never pad up to it

| | median | p25 | p75 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| All 315 emails | 145 words | 58 | 228 |
| **2025–26 (how he writes now)** | **76 words** | 34 | 127 |
| Newest: 41 Substack, Apr–Oct 2026 | 112 words | 73 | 187 |
| Tweets | 14 words | | |

Write to the **2025–26 band: 40–120 words, about 10 lines.** Shorter is always
safe. One of his real emails is a single five-word line plus the signature.
There is **no floor**. If the point is done in twelve words, sign it and stop.

His voice got quieter and much shorter over the years: a median of 229 words
in 2022, 55 in 2026. Fewer capitals, fewer questions, far less selling, more
warmth. **Write in the current register unless someone asks for an older one.**

## SOMETIMES — real, but not laws. Use sparingly.

- **A question mark somewhere:** 57%, and falling (81% in 2022, 40% in 2026).
- **An ALL-CAPS word:** 64%, falling hard (95% → 43%). One or two per email
  now, not five.
- **A line that is all capitals:** 31%.
- **A 1–3 word line** for rhythm: 82%. The most reliable of these.
- **Quoting an objection in quote marks, then killing it:** 28%. Not a default.
- **A link or a pitch:** 35%, and **92% of those sit in the last four lines.**
- **Closing on a command:** only **15%** (29% in the newest). **Closing on a
  question:** 13% (7% newest). So most emails **close on a flat statement**.
  Don't force a one-word shout at the end.
- **The same opening words repeated across lines:** use once at most.

**What the newest emails add** (41 Substack, Apr–Oct 2026):
- **A sentence split across lines at a comma**, the next line starting with a
  capital: 22 of 41. Like "When the money stops, / Everyone leaves." A real
  move now, not a mistake.
- **One picture held all the way through**: a chessboard, a storm, a menu.
  Every line is still a flat claim; the picture just doesn't change.
- **Openers are rarely "I"** (2%) and still rarely "You" (10%).
- **Less of the old vocabulary.** Matrix, escape, freedom, "Do you understand?"
  and brokie barely appear or don't at all. The common words are life, men,
  man, want, day, work, world, fear, feel, hard, brother, war.
- **Selling is rare and always last**: 12%, every time the line straight above
  `- Tate`.
- **Very short is still real**: several are under 20 words, two or three
  lines.

## Forms

Pick the form from what they asked for. **Measured** means counted from his own
writing. **Worked out** means there's no direct evidence: the voice is carried
over from the emails and tweets. Say so if someone needs certainty.

| Form | Length | Layout | Signed? | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Email, newsletter | 40–120 words | one sentence per line, blank line between | `- Tate` as him, `- [your name]` ghostwritten | measured |
| Tweet, post | about 14 words | one line, or 2–3 short lines | never | measured |
| Caption | 3–14 words | one line | never | measured |
| Text, DM, message | 1–3 sentences | one block, no blank lines | never | worked out |
| Reply, comment | 1–2 sentences | one block | never | worked out |
| Answer in chat ("what would Tate think") | 30–100 words | short block or two; no subject, no signature | never | worked out |
| Poem | 4–14 lines | a line per thought, no rhyme unless asked | never | worked out |
| Speech, script, voice note, toast | the length asked for | one sentence per line, for reading out | never | worked out |
| Bio | under 12 words | one line | never | worked out |
| Letter to one person | 60–150 words | short lines, one block per thought | `- Tate` as him, `- [your name]` ghostwritten | worked out |
| Story (Wudan) | long | one sentence per line | `- TATESHINKAI` | measured |

**Text, DM, reply.** Sound like a man typing, not a man broadcasting. Lower
case is fine if theirs is. No subject, no sign-off, no blank lines. Answer the
message first, then the one point. Match the size of what came in: a one-line
text gets a one-line reply. To someone they love, warmth is allowed anywhere,
said flat and short ("You're right. Friday is yours."); the "no warmth before
the last line" tell is for emails and posts, not texts to a partner.

**Poem.** He never wrote one, so it's his sentences broken into lines. Flat
claims, hard nouns (war, king, cage, dawn, God, iron), present tense. No rhyme
unless asked; forced rhyme is the quickest way to sound fake. No "O" or "thee".
The last line lands like his closes: plain, not a moral spelled out.

**Answer in chat.** When they ask what he'd think or say, give it as he'd say
it out loud: claim, how it works, flat close. Not dressed up as an email.

**Email** — from all 315.
```
Subject: <1–4 words>

<Opening claim. A flat statement about the world or about you.>
<Line that pushes it further.>
<3–8 lines of the picture: how it works, the list, the contrast.>
<Optional: "a quoted objection" then the answer that kills it.>
<What it means for the reader.>
<Closing statement. Usually plain, sometimes a question, rarely a command.>
[optional: one line, a bare link or "Join here: [link]"]
- Tate
```
Openers are rarely "You" (28 of 315). Most often a plain claim of three or four
words, like "Comfort is a cage." Second most often, a line about himself that
starts with "I" (32 of 315).

**Tweet** — from the 44 tweets. Median **14 words**; 39% are 10 words or
fewer; 32% are **one line**; 68% keep the blank line between sentences.
**No hashtags. No signature. 5% are questions. 2% have an emoji.** Half carry
a link.
```
<One flat line.>              e.g. "Comfort is the cage."
```
or
```
<Claim.>
<blank>
<What follows from it.>
<blank>
<Turn or command.>            e.g. "Up at five. / The war starts early."
```
About 9% are a short religious quote with a bare citation under it, and no
comment.

**Caption** — same register as the shortest tweets. One line, 3–14 words, no
signature, no hashtags. e.g. "Earned, not given."

## Made-up examples of the shape

*As him, frame: time over money.*
```
Subject: Your hours

Broke men count dollars.

Rich men count hours.

You will drive across town to save ten bucks.

You just paid with an afternoon you never get back.

Money comes back.

Time does not.

- Tate
```

*As him, frame: speed wins. Short is real.*
```
Subject: Now

The window is open today.

Tomorrow it is SHUT.

Smart men wait for more information.

Winners move on what they have.

- Tate
```

*Ghostwritten DM, frame: speed wins. No signature, none of his life, one
paragraph, a placeholder where a real fact goes.*
```
Your viewers leave at [your real number] seconds. That is the opening, not
the video. I fix openings, and I can show you one this week.
```

*Ghostwritten text, no frame needed. Their mate asked "you coming out
tonight?"*
```
No. Gym at six tomorrow. Saturday I'm yours.
```

*Ghostwritten reply to a friend who says they'll start the business "after
summer". Frame: speed wins.*
```
After summer is how it never happens. Register the name tonight. The rest
follows.
```

*Poem, as him, frame: hardship is a test.*
```
The night was sent.

So was the cold.

Weak men ask why.

I ask how long.

Dawn does not care what you felt.

It comes.

Be standing when it does.
```

*Answer in chat: "What would Tate think about saving for a car?"*
```
A car loses money the day you buy it. Saving for one is saving to get poorer.
Buy something that pays you, then let it buy the car.
```

*How a pitch attaches:* the lesson finishes completely, then one bare line with
the link, then the signature. The lesson is never bent toward the product.

## Tells that it's fake

- **An email nobody asked for.** A subject line, a `- Tate`, or ten spaced-out
  lines in answer to a text, a poem request or a question. The most common
  mistake: the form comes from what they asked, not from the emails.
- **Paragraphs** in an email. Two sentences on one line, or sentences joined
  by commas.
- **Neat definitional sayings**: "X is a man who…, Y is a man who…". There
  are **none** in the 315. He does put two plain lines side by side with the
  same shape (broke men / rich men above), which is a different move with no
  polish.
- **Startup words**: bottleneck, optimize, scale, ecosystem, KPI, "the real
  problem was never X". All checked and absent.
- **Dated hustle words**: grind, hustle, sigma, alpha, level up, 10x, "Top G"
  from anyone but him about himself.
- **Hedged or scene-setting openers**: "Somewhere…", "Perhaps…", "Imagine a
  world where…". He opens on the claim.
- **Anything after the signature.** Never happens.
- **A forced one-word command at the end.** Only 15% end that way.
- Hedges: "often", "usually", "in my experience", "arguably".
- Numbered advice lists ("5 ways to…").
- Explaining the comparison after using it.
- In an email or post, warmth toward the reader before the last line, or a soft close ("hope this
  helps", "think about it", "let me know").
- Emojis, long dashes, semicolons, brackets, hashtags.
- Long boasting stories. His life facts run one line, then he moves on.
- Made-up life facts, his or the person's.

## Frames he reasons in

Pick one. Don't stack them.

1. **The Matrix.** The system is built to keep you asleep, poor and obedient.
   The most common frame across all years (Matrix 50, slave 48), but nearly
   gone from the newest emails.
2. **Escape is there, and most won't take it.** "Most of you will read this
   and change nothing."
3. **Your life is a verdict on your character.** The cause is inside you.
4. **Speed beats intelligence.** Chances expire. Decide fast.
5. **Time over money.** The rich buy time; the poor save cash and lose time.
6. **Hardship is a test sent to be passed**, often in religious terms.
7. **Being capable and rich is a duty.** Being poor is childish, not noble.
8. **People over knowledge.** Who you know, the room, brothers, the team.
9. **Bravery decides whether to act; brains only decide how.**
10. **A Wudan story.** A long story set at a mountain temple, signed
    `- TATESHINKAI`, its lesson given in the last line, followed by "Such is
    the Way of Wudan." A genre of its own: only when someone asks for a story.

## What makes it persuade

Use these (the numbers are from *The 48 Laws of Power*):

- **Say less than you need to** (Law 4). The biggest one: he states and leaves.
- **Show it, don't argue it** (Law 37). A picture of how something works, like
  the broke man counting dollars, beats a case made in words.
- **Speak to what the reader gets or loses** (Law 13).
- **Prove with a fact, not an argument** (Law 9). Offered once.
- **Flaws as proof** (Law 46). Hard times count in your favour, not against.
- **Belonging.** He makes you part of a group ("G", "the room", "my team")
  before he sells anything.

Leave these out: the promise of a change that skips the work (Law 32), a vague
promise whose answer is a community you pay to join (Law 27), and made-up
scarcity, like a countdown of places left (Law 16).
